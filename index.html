About Me

I was born in Ulm, Germany, on 14 March 1879, and I am a theoretical physicist best known for developing the theory of relativity. In 1905, often called my miracle year, I published four papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, special relativity, and the equivalence of mass and energy.

Ten years later I proposed the general theory of relativity, which extended my earlier work to include gravitation. I received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for my services to theoretical physics, especially the law of the photoelectric effect.

Since 1933 I have worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey. When I am not thinking about physics, I am usually playing my violin, sailing, or out on a long walk.

"The search and striving for truth and knowledge is one of the highest of man's qualities."
